# Public Voice Between Elections
## A discussion pack for civic and activist groups in India
100 public-interest issues • practical routes for participation • existing organisations and remote contributions
Draft v0.1 | 13 October 2026 | Prepared for discussion, correction and possible collaboration
This is a proposal, not an endorsed movement platform. No organisation listed here has agreed to participate. The specific movement or coalition has not been identified; affiliations are therefore not asserted.
# 1\. Why this proposal exists
A vote should not be the last time the public is heard. People need ways to raise an issue, obtain a competent answer, challenge refusal and verify what changed—between elections and across constituency boundaries.
We propose a continuous participation process built around four questions: Who can raise an issue? Who must answer? What can change? What happens when power refuses to listen?
Territory identifies some public responsibilities. It should not monopolise voice on harms that cross territories. A migrant, a teacher, a patient or a worker should be able to contribute from where they are. Remote participation can support on-ground organising without replacing affected people’s leadership.
The 100 issue families are a starting agenda for deliberation, not a nationally validated ranking or a claim that every problem is present everywhere. Issue numbers are stable references. Local groups should correct, split, merge or add issues based on evidence.
Government can reject a preferred policy with defensible reasons. It should not evade an issue through silence, endless transfer, unexplained closure or a promise labelled as a completed remedy.
# 2\. The public commitments we want to test
  - Open intake: people can raise concerns through assisted, offline, telephone, postal and accessible digital channels. No mandatory Aadhaar, smartphone or English proficiency.
  - Named responsibility: every case has a verified lead office, relevant co-owners, applicable rules and an accountable response date.
  - Reasoned response: explain evidence, jurisdiction, proposed action or refusal, and how to challenge the decision.
  - Affected-person deliberation: serious systemic issues receive a fair hearing, including counterarguments and preserved dissent.
  - Evidence of change: distinguish action promised, action completed and outcome independently verified.
  - Protection: collect only necessary personal information; obtain consent for attribution; protect people from exposure and retaliation.
  - Rights before popularity: low-volume harms are not discarded because they lack signatures, followers or media attention.
These are proposed commitments requiring adoption. They are not existing universal legal guarantees. Electoral participation, RTI eligibility, statutory appeals and institutional mandates retain their own rules.
# 3\. Three routes, with different outcomes
## Individual remedy
Example: a household is denied an existing ration entitlement. The expected result is a lawful case decision, correction or reasoned refusal and a usable appeal. A public debate must not delay an available individual remedy.
## Systemic review
Example: repeated authentication failures prevent access to a public scheme. The expected result is evidence gathering, affected-person participation, alternative designs, a decision and an implementation review.
## Policy or law
Example: extend portable voting or require public hearings. The expected result is a consultation, explicit tradeoffs, a reasoned institutional response and the appropriate rule-making or legislative process. A poll does not create legal authority.
Immediate safety threats need appropriate emergency or protection services. This project is not an emergency responder. Filing here must not make people miss statutory appeal or litigation deadlines.
# 4\. Existing foundations and proposed additions
Existing: CPGRAMS/state grievance routes where admissible; departmental and statutory appeals; RTI for existing records; eligible parliamentary petitions; regulators and rights bodies within their mandates; qualified legal assistance and courts/tribunals under applicable procedure.
DARPG’s August 2024 grievance guidelines generally use a 21-day framework with interim explanations where additional time is needed. RTI ordinarily has a 30-day response rule and a 48-hour life-or-liberty information provision, subject to the Act. Neither is a universal guarantee of a public hearing or policy change. \[Sources 1–3\]
Proposed additions: an accessible cross-channel intake record; verified ownership; a systemic-issue hearing pathway; public reasons for admission/refusal; implementation evidence; independent review; and preserved dissent.
Suggested process targets: receipt immediately digitally or within two working days with assistance; routing within seven calendar days; ordinary response within 21 calendar days unless governing law specifies otherwise; systemic admission decision within 30 days; hearing/decision normally within 60/90 days of admission. These are design targets. Applicable law and urgent safety procedures take precedence.
# 5\. When power refuses to listen
  - Silence: preserve filing time, notify the responsible supervisor and enable review; record overdue status safely.
  - Wrong office: coordinate a verified transfer; do not call a referral a resolved problem.
  - Boilerplate closure: request case-specific reasons, evidence and appeal information; preserve the challenge.
  - Denied entitlement: use the applicable administrative, specialist or legal remedy after checking jurisdiction and deadlines.
  - Systemic failure: seek a hearing, representative scrutiny, eligible petition or audit review; retain lawful organising outside the platform.
  - Retaliation or credible harm: use protected referrals and appropriate rights/legal support; do not expose the person on a dashboard.
  - Alleged corruption: preserve evidence and use competent vigilance/investigation channels. Allegations are not findings of guilt.
No automatic fine, removal, court order or mandatory hearing is invented by this proposal. Any new enforceable consequence requires lawful authority and due process. Peaceful collective action, journalism and association must remain available outside the software.
# 6\. A useful first contribution—without attending a street action
An existing organisation need not become a coalition member to improve this draft. A bounded technical correction, an anonymised case example or a review of one issue family is useful. Remote contribution is an option, not evidence that a group is inactive or stays indoors.
  - Evidence: verify one public claim, identify its source and denominator, and record what remains uncertain.
  - Law and jurisdiction: correct one owner/remedy map and explain the governing procedure.
  - Research and data: audit public records, clean datasets or examine repeated service failures without exposing personal identities.
  - Language and communication: translate one issue brief into Marathi, Hindi or another relevant language; check meaning with users.
  - Accessibility: review an intake journey or document with disabled participants and compensate specialist review where possible.
  - Design and software: test a synthetic intake/appeal workflow for routing, privacy, failure recovery and harmful automation.
  - Deliberation: facilitate a small remote session that records evidence, counterarguments, agreement and dissent.
  - On-ground connection: ask affected people what the draft misunderstands; relay their corrections with consent and attribution preferences.
A simple contribution form: issue ID; proposed correction/task; evidence link; method; effort and availability chosen by the contributor; privacy needs; reviewer; expected output; credit/consent preference. Do not imply that a volunteer promises unpaid professional legal representation or indefinite support.

# 8\. A short invitation groups can adapt
We are developing a public-interest framework for continuous democratic participation in India: who can raise an issue, who must answer, what can change, and how refusal can be challenged. The draft maps 100 issue families and separates existing remedies from proposed reforms.
Would your group review one issue family or contribute one bounded task—evidence checking, legal mapping, translation, accessibility, data, design or deliberation? Remote contribution is welcome. Critical feedback is as useful as agreement. Reviewing the draft does not imply endorsement, coalition membership or permission to use your name.
Please tell us what you would correct, what you could contribute, and any privacy, credit or capacity conditions. We will agree the scope before treating it as a commitment.
# 9\. First organising cycle
Choose three contrasting pilot issues with affected people: a basic service, an entitlement exclusion and a transparency question. The choice is a practical test, not a severity ranking.
For each, prepare a one-page issue brief with a verified owner, relevant procedure, evidence, counterargument, proposed change and safe challenge route. Invite a small set of relevant groups to correct it. Record agreement, dissent and scope; obtain consent before publishing names.
Test the process with synthetic records before handling sensitive complaints. Agree independent review and safe data handling. Then assess whether responses and outcomes improve, not merely whether the portal attracts activity.
Do not collect a movement-membership registry by inference. Only record explicit consent, scope and minimum necessary contact details in a separately controlled organising record. This discussion pack contains no private complainant data.

# Appendix B. What the challenge routes mean
## A — Administrative review
Relevant service complaint, supervisor, statutory departmental appeal or CPGRAMS/state system where admissible. Check exclusions and deadlines.
## I — Information / RTI
Existing records through lawful disclosure or RTI for eligible citizens, with appropriate appeals. Information access does not itself enforce a remedy.
## R — Regulator or specialist redress
Competent regulator, ombudsman or statutory grievance body only within its remit and after applicable prerequisites.
## H — Rights or protection route
Appropriate commission, protection service or specialist authority, subject to mandate and confidentiality. A recommendation is not necessarily a binding executable order.
## J — Qualified legal review / competent court or tribunal
Qualified advice, eligible legal aid and proper judicial/tribunal procedure. No guaranteed admission, relief or timing.
## P — Policy hearing / legislative scrutiny / lawful advocacy
Proposed affected-person hearing, eligible petition, representative scrutiny, consultation or lawful advocacy. Automatic hearings require institutional adoption.
## V — Vigilance or audit referral
Competent vigilance, anti-corruption, social-audit or audit process. A referral is not a finding of guilt or a guarantee of compensation.
These routes are options, not a compulsory ladder. Urgent protection and individual relief must not wait for a public hearing.
