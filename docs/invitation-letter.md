# Invitation letter

Section 8 of the pack is a short invitation groups can adapt. The text below is reproduced verbatim.
A short preamble you can adapt sits above it, and the three questions the invitation asks are set
out after it so the reply can be structured.

Before sending: no organisation listed in the pack has agreed to participate, and sending this
letter does not change that. Use the organisation's public institutional route, not a personal
contact. Agree scope before treating any reply as a commitment.

## Adaptable preamble

> Dear [organisation or team],
>
> We are writing from [group], a [one line: who you are and where]. We are reviewing a draft
> discussion pack, *Public Voice Between Elections* (draft v0.1, 13 October 2026), and your public
> work on [documented relevance, as the pack states it] is relevant to [issue id(s) and title(s)].
> The pack and its data are open at [repository or site link]. The invitation below is the pack's
> own text.

Replace every bracket. Do not add claims about the organisation beyond its own public pages.

## The invitation (section 8, verbatim)

> We are developing a public-interest framework for continuous democratic participation in India:
> who can raise an issue, who must answer, what can change, and how refusal can be challenged. The
> draft maps 100 issue families and separates existing remedies from proposed reforms.
>
> Would your group review one issue family or contribute one bounded task—evidence checking, legal
> mapping, translation, accessibility, data, design or deliberation? Remote contribution is welcome.
> Critical feedback is as useful as agreement. Reviewing the draft does not imply endorsement,
> coalition membership or permission to use your name.
>
> Please tell us what you would correct, what you could contribute, and any privacy, credit or
> capacity conditions. We will agree the scope before treating it as a commitment.

## The three questions

The last paragraph asks the group three things. Offer them as headings so the reply is easy to
record:

1. **What would you correct?** Any issue card, owner map, route, claim or wording. A correction is
   as welcome as a contribution.
2. **What could you contribute?** One bounded task from section 6: evidence, law and jurisdiction,
   research and data, language and communication, accessibility, design and software, deliberation,
   or on-ground connection. Effort and availability are chosen by the contributor.
3. **What are your privacy, credit or capacity conditions?** Whether and how the group wants to be
   named, what it will not do, and how long it is available. Nothing is treated as a commitment
   until scope is agreed.

## After a reply

- Record the reply's answers to the three questions in the organising record, with the consent and
  attribution preference stated. Do not collect a movement-membership registry by inference.
- Open the matching issue form for each correction or task.
- Do not list the organisation as participating, anywhere, until it has agreed to that in writing.
